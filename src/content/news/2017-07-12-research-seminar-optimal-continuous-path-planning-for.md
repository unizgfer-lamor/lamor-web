**Abstract:**

Path planning in a known environment with obstacles presented by environment map is very common task in mobile robot applications and has been widely studied. The most commonly used discrete search approaches (such as Dijkstra, A star, D star, etc.) return path which consists of straight-lines path connecting the cells centers towards the goal location. These paths do not have continuous angular velocity profile and therefore need to be pre-smoothed to become feasible for the wheeled robot to follow it.\

Continuous approaches on the other side combine some motion primitives to directly obtain smooth drivable path for a wheeled robot. In this presentation continuous path is composed of fifth order Bernstein-Bézier curves with continuous velocities and curvature transitions. The obtained path can therefore be directly driven by wheeled mobile robots. The shortest path is then obtained by appropriate composition of the Bézier curve sections which is done by hybrid A star search algorithm. To achieve the shortest travelling time optimal velocity profile determination is included by considering robot capabilities such as maximal allowed accelerations and velocity. Basic idea will be presented with several path planning examples.

**Bio:**\
Gregor Klančar received B.Sc. and Ph.D. in electrical engineering from the University of Ljubljana, Slovenia in 1999 and 2003 respectively. He is currently employed as an assistant at the Faculty of Electrical Engineering at the University of Ljubljana. His research interests are autonomous mobile robots, their motion control, trajectory tracking, path planning, localization and supervision of multiagent systems.
